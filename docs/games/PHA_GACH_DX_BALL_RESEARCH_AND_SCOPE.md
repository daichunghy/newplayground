## Source choice and edition uncertainty

This candidate implements the familiar paddle-and-ball brick-breaking loop. Atari's official Breakout description says to move a paddle along the bottom, hit a ball into a brick wall, and score as bricks disappear. That is the source for the version-independent loop, not a claim that this is an Atari Breakout port. [Atari Breakout](https://atari.com/pages/breakout)

For DX-Ball history, Michael P. Welch's archived developer page says he first released DX-Ball in 1996, dates the Classic DX-Ball 1.09 update to 1998-12-20, and dates DX-Ball 2's release to 1998-12-21. It also mentions pausing, faster-ball brick scoring, and power-ups. [Welch archive](https://classic.blitwise.com/Scorched_Reality/index.html)

The detailed DX-Ball 1 rules available in this review come from a first-party v1.07 readme mirrored by a third party, not from a currently official-hosted readme. That document describes keeping one or more balls in play with a paddle, losing a life when all balls fall, clearing bricks to score and advance boards, and blue/red/grey bonus categories. Its version/date and package terms are not evidence that every 1.09 build or later port behaved the same way. The v1.07 readme and Welch's v1.09 archive therefore leave edition-specific details uncertain. [Mirrored v1.07 readme](https://oldgamesdownload.com/readme/dx-ball-windows-readme-english/)

DX-Ball 2 material is treated only as sequel evidence. Longbow's official page describes 150 boards, 20 classic power-ups, difficulty settings, and a Classic mode; those features are not assumed to define DX-Ball 1. [Longbow DX-Ball 2](https://longbowgames.com/dxball2/)

## Original candidate rules

- Move one paddle with left/right arrows, A/D, mouse movement, or touch drag. A first touch/click or Space launches the ball; after a lost life, touch or Space serves again.
- Bounce the ball off the paddle and side/top walls into a single-hit brick field. A brick is worth 10 points. Missing the paddle costs one of three lives.
- Clear a field to advance. Four candidate-authored geometric patterns rotate as the levels continue; ball speed rises by level and caps at a fixed maximum.
- Pause with P or the pause control. Restart with the restart control. Window blur pauses active play.
- There are no power-ups, multi-ball, catch/laser paddle, copied board sets, score-speed bonuses, persistent score, or lives-as-paddles claim. Those omissions keep the first build small and distinct.
- The model uses a 120 Hz fixed simulation step, a bounded elapsed-time accumulator, deterministic level patterns, and no random or network state.

These are implementation choices for this candidate. They do not claim parity with a specific DX-Ball edition.

## Rights and asset provenance

The mirrored v1.07 readme identifies DX-Ball as ©1996 Michael P. Welch and permits copying/distribution only of the unmodified zipped package; it forbids modifying the package and bundling it in commercial products. Its statement that that version's music/effects came from the public domain does not identify the files or settle rights for other editions. This candidate uses no original package, code, board, logo, image, music, or sampled sound.

The engine code is new. Brick patterns are generated by this candidate's small geometric formulas. Canvas art is drawn from basic shapes and gradients. `assets/pha-gach-original.svg` is a new vector cover. Sound effects use short locally synthesized tones through the repository audio engine; there is no music track or downloaded audio.

The U.S. Copyright Office says game ideas, titles, and methods of play aren't copyright-protected, while expressive text and artwork may be; its computer-program guidance also excludes ideas, logic, algorithms, methods, and layouts from program copyright. This is U.S.-specific general guidance, not a legal conclusion about any particular implementation. ## Integration and acceptance

Files include `scripts/games/dx-ball-model.js`, `scripts/games/dx-ball.js`, `scripts/games/dx-ball.css`, `assets/pha-gach-original.svg`, focused model/UI tests, and this dossier. The module is wired locally to the retained catalog route with a project-authored cover and manifest entry.

The local catalog route loads the model before the view, includes the scoped CSS, and mounts through the existing session listener/RAF/cleanup contract. The visible game and card title are generic Phá Gạch; the historical ID and DX-Ball reference remain documented only as unresolved provenance.

Fourteen focused model/UI tests cover deterministic stepping, wall/paddle/brick/life flow, level progression, keyboard/touch input, exact catalog routing, pause/restart, blur pause, and teardown. These doubles do not verify actual browser layout, Canvas rendering, touch hardware, or audio hardware. Browser/device playtesting and catalog/title clearance remain acceptance work.
